Part 1 · Why our own platform

Our box. Our data. Our rules.

Vercel stays fine for prototypes. But team tools touch real company and client data — so they run on taincap, a server we operate, with Dokploy (an open-source PaaS) on top and plain Docker underneath: anything that runs in a container runs here.

Reason 01

Own the data.

On a hosted PaaS, your data lives on someone else's cloud, under someone else's policies. On our box, it never leaves infrastructure we control — full stop.

Reason 02

Secure everything.

Every app sits behind the company identity wall before a single request lands. The perimeter is ours — not a vendor feature we hope is configured right.

Reason 03

Control all the things.

Server, proxy, databases, access policies — ours to inspect, configure, and change. When something matters, we don't file a ticket. We go look.

One hard boundary Internal-facing only. Might a customer ever touch it? Then it's engineering's stack and a conversation first — never a quiet deploy. The wall (two slides away) makes this physical.

Part 3 · Cloudflare Zero Trust

The wall.

Every request to every *.anduincapital.dev host is checked at Cloudflare's edge — before our server ever sees it. No company SSO session? It never arrives. That's "private by default" — and it cuts two ways:

Humans

Sail through.

A browser signs in once, gets a cookie, and passes invisibly from then on. Every app you ship today is instantly company-only — zero perimeter code.

Machines

Hit the wall.

A curl, a CI job, an MCP server, a Slack webhook — no browser, no company account. A 302 to a login page it can never solve. Fails silently.

Two consequences:third-party webhooks die here — design for pollingClaude needs through — your machine gets an identity: WARP →

🔧 Lab 1a · 5 min — Cloudflare WARP

Already on your machine. Verify the team.

  1. Find the ☁️ in your menu bar — WARP is already installed on team machines. (Truly missing? brew install --cask cloudflare-warp.)
  2. Open it → Profile → Team name must say anduin → and flip the toggle to Connected. Your device's traffic now carries your company identity — every process on it, Claude included.
  3. No team / not signed in? Preferences → Account → Login with Cloudflare Zero Trust → team anduin → company Google → approve.

No tokens to mint, nothing expires mid-demo. One toggle — and it's the first thing to check if Dokploy calls ever return an HTML sign-in page.

Deploy it — by saying so.

  1. Paste the deploy prompt from your sheet — it names your project, repo, and subdomain.
  2. Claude will STOP and ask where your database lives. Answer: “create a new managed Postgres in my project.” That's not workshop debris — it's your instance, shared by every app you ship after today.

  4. Build minutes = quiz minutes: why is HTTPS off at the domain? why is the app on two networks? migrate says “retrying” — failure? (No — it's waiting for the DB.)

Take-home · your app, this week

Now do it with your app.

1
Containerize it — run dokploy-starter in absorb mode: it lifts the patterns (compose file, networks, migrate one-shot) into your code.
2
Push to a private repo this time — which means a deploy key. The skill automates it; just know GitHub silently drops keys if a repo is ever transferred — first suspect when a deploy dies at clone.
3
Say the words: deploy this to dokploy. Answer the database question differently now: “reuse my Postgres” — the one you made today.
4
Expect the OAuth reminder if you have Google sign-in — origins & redirect URIs are Console-only, forever manual. Claude hands you the exact values.
5
The sharp edge: no third-party webhooks through the wall. Poll (the Cauldron shape), or open the scoped-bypass conversation with the box owner early.
6
Day 2: ship v2 with push, then “redeploy it”. Something's weird? “show me my app's logs” — Claude reads them over MCP. WARP toggle first if calls bounce.
